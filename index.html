Dissertation

My PhD thesis focuses on knowledge management in CAE, specifically in the field of simulation-based crashworthiness design. As virtual testing continues to expand, both the size of individual simulation models and the number of simulations across different load cases, vehicle variants and levels of detail continue to grow. Combined with the highly nonlinear behaviour of vehicle structures under crash loading, this results in increasingly large and complex datasets that are difficult to analyse and utilize systematically. My goal is to address these challenges by applying data analysis methods to structure, analyse and connect simulation data, making engineering knowledge easier to extract, reuse and leverage in the design process.

ComplicationImpact

The structure deforms

The crash boxes fold, one longitudinal rail buckles and the hood bulges. Forces flow along three load paths; the colour contour shows the plastic strain (schematic).

  • Load path 1 · crash box → longitudinal rail → rocker
  • Load path 2 · upper rail (shotgun) → A-pillar → roof
  • Load path 3 · subframe → tunnel
ComplicationNonlinearity 1/4

Elastic-plastic material behaviour

The material is loaded beyond its yield point: on the flow curve, the state moves from the elastic into the plastic range – the deformation remains.

ComplicationNonlinearity 2/4

Buckling of profiles

Under axial compression, one longitudinal rail buckles to one side: a plastic hinge forms – a single kink instead of even folding. Small imperfections decide whether and where this happens.

ComplicationNonlinearity 3/4

Material failure

At the joint between longitudinal rail and rocker, elements exceed their failure limit and are deleted – a crack runs around the profile and the load path is interrupted.

ComplicationNonlinearity 4/4

Component collisions

Late in the crash, the engine tilts backwards. The brake booster tilts and strikes the firewall with its edge. This causes a local intrusion effect.

ComplicationOverview

Everything happens at once

This makes predictions very difficult: there are countless effects and interactions in the structure – and they influence each other.

SolutionData lake

The data is already there

We have vast amounts of simulation data – mostly stored in data lakes – and a large toolbox of machine learning and data analysis methods.

Unlocking the treasure

Now it's time to unlock this treasure with new methods of data analysis and AI – and to establish data-driven development processes.

We don't lack data – and the expert knowledge to feed AI assistants is buried in it.

Anomaly detection

Learn what is normal – flag what is not

Each point is one simulation run (schematic). Clusters are the typical deformation modes; runs that deviate are flagged automatically – this is exactly where the knowledge hides.

  • normal: known deformation modes
  • slightly unusual
  • anomaly: significant increase in intrusion, unexpected buckling, structural integrity

A Knowledge Graph of engineering experience

The key step is what happens next: these individual observations are not stored as isolated records. They are connected in a Knowledge Graph. Over time, the graph builds relationships between components, modifications, physical effects, load cases, model variants and previous engineering decisions.

In this way, thousands of individual simulation runs gradually form a connected representation of engineering experience. This creates a particularly powerful foundation for AI.
